# Prepaid Local SIM vs eSIM: Which Actually Makes Sense for Your Trip

The prepaid-SIM-versus-eSIM decision gets treated as a settled question online ("eSIM is just better now"), but the right answer actually depends heavily on trip length, destination, and what you are doing with the connection — a weekend trip and a multi-month stay under a [digital nomad visa](https://nomadbriefing.com/digital-nomad-visa/japan/) are genuinely different problems.

## Short trips usually favor an eSIM, but check the data allowance math first

For a trip of a week or two, an eSIM installed before departure avoids the hassle of finding a local shop and speaking through a language barrier for a physical SIM. But travel eSIM plans are frequently priced by fixed data buckets rather than unlimited local rates, so check the actual gigabyte allowance against expected usage before assuming it beats a local SIM on cost — see our full [eSIM guide](https://nomadbriefing.com/esim/) for how these plans are typically structured.

## Long stays often favor a local physical SIM on cost grounds

For a stay of a month or more, a local carrier's prepaid SIM, bought in-country, is frequently cheaper per gigabyte than an international eSIM plan, and it also gives you a local phone number, which some banking apps, delivery services, and government portals in destinations like [Thailand](https://nomadbriefing.com/thailand/) or [Portugal](https://nomadbriefing.com/portugal/) may actually require for SMS verification.

## Dual-SIM or eSIM-plus-physical-SIM setups solve the number problem

A common middle path for a longer stay: keep your home number reachable on an eSIM for calls and verification codes tied to home-country accounts, while using a cheap local physical SIM as the primary data line. This requires a phone that actually supports dual SIM (physical plus eSIM simultaneously), which is worth confirming before you travel, not after arriving somewhere with limited return/exchange options.

## Device compatibility quietly rules out eSIM for some travelers

Not every phone, including some phones legitimately sold as "unlocked," has an activated eSIM chip or carrier-unlocked eSIM support — this varies by exact model and sometimes by which regional version of a phone you have. If you have not confirmed compatibility, a physical SIM is the safer fallback regardless of which option would otherwise be cheaper.

## Reactivation and top-up friction differs sharply

Local prepaid SIMs sometimes require in-country ID verification to reactivate after they lapse, which can be inconvenient if you leave and return to a destination months apart. eSIM travel plans are generally easier to simply repurchase from anywhere, which matters for people who move between countries repeatedly rather than staying put.

## The right call depends on the specific stay, not a blanket rule

There is no single correct answer between the two — the right choice depends on trip length, destination-specific SIM registration rules, and whether local numbers matter for accounts you need to access, which is worth checking against destination-specific guides like our timing research for [Japan](https://nomadbriefing.com/best-time-to-visit/japan/) or general planning notes for [Mexico](https://nomadbriefing.com/mexico/) rather than assuming one format fits every trip.
